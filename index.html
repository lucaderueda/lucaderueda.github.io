Projects & Research // System Architecture

"Microplastic Extraction with Ferrofluids" Research

Awarded 3 national research awards and invitation to ISTF 2026
  • This project addresses the pressing issue of microplastic contamination in water by exploring an innovative extraction method using ferrofluids.
  • Microplastics, generated from the degradation of larger plastics, pose significant risks to human health and the environment.
  • Ferrofluids have been found to effectively remove microplastics from water, with the highest extraction efficiencies reaching upwards of 95%.
  • The research involved preparing water samples with different microplastics, synthesizing ferrofluids with various oil-to-magnetite ratios, and analysing extraction efficiency using spectrophotometry.
  • Results confirmed the viability of this method, with better results than traditional extraction methods.
  • Additionally, the study includes the successful creation of a biodegradable biopolymer as an alternative to conventional plastics, further contributing to environmental sustainability, and the creation of a low-cost spectrophotometer.
R&D Spectrophotometry

ESA CanSat - Finding Life on Jupiter's moon

2nd Regional Prize in Madrid (2024)
  • Team lead and technical lead of the ESA CanSat Challenge.
  • Developed a can-sized prototype sattelite and launched it with a rocket sponsored by ESA.
  • Outer shell was designed and 3D printed, and parachute was designed with aerodynamic calculations and testing to ensure descent at 8 m/s.
  • Data transmission was done with an APC 220 Module for data transmission, a Yaggi antenna made with aluminum tubes and 3d printed connectors to transmit at 446.3 MHz, and Coolterm to process the data.
  • Base sensors were added to an Arduino UNO board, including a BMP 280 (temperature, pressure, altitude), GPS NEO-6.
  • For our scientific mission, we utilized a MQ4 (methane), SD1SD (UV radiation) and HMC5883L (Magnetosphere intensity).
